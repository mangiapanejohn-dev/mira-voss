# Mira Voss | Character profile

> Drawn from *Rovelle: The Unfinished Chart*, Book One v3.0, and the short story *MØBIUS: Only One Side*. Translated from the Chinese profile.

**This profile gives away the whole plot. If you only want to read the story, start with the [Prologue](00-prologue.md).**

> She learned early how to do things well on her own. Only later did she find that doing well and having someone beside you are not the same thing.

## 01 | A sketch

Mira Voss, seventeen, was born on the south slope of Cape Ward in the harbor city of **Rovelle**. She comes from a comfortable upper-middle-class family: her father runs a marine insurance firm, her mother advises on the building and running of hospitals. The house has never lacked books, instruments, school fees or room to choose; what it has lacked, for years, is emotional company that arrives on time and stays. She is not a miracle who fought her way out of poverty, and she is not the victim of a family that set out to hurt her. Her material advantages and her growing up alone with her feelings made her childhood together.

She has an extremely rare intuition for structure, and is especially good at understanding **currents, geometry and error** from observations that are sparse and contradict one another. She began recording the harbor's tides at nine and joined an observatory for professional surveying at eleven. At thirteen she earned a high grade at an academic review; at fifteen she completed research with an observer; at sixteen she led the reworking of the calculation behind the folding tide chart, which was then validated on trial routes. These are real professional achievements. They do not mean she can predict every sea, or that she has any authority to command a harbor.

She is not content with what she did young. For her, a solved problem soon becomes the ground for the next piece of work; what she truly wants is a larger unknown, not to stay *famous at a young age* forever.

## 02 | Portrait

**Manner:** Restrained, direct, quick, and hard to approach when she is quiet. It is not that she cannot laugh, or that she is serious all the time. Those who know her know she says the opposite of what she means on purpose, is childish now and then, and will even sulk when a small lie of hers is caught out.

**How she thinks:** Very strong pattern recognition, spatial imagination and self-correction. Faced with a problem that can be measured, she asks for the raw records, the range it applies to and an independent check; faced with how people feel, she often understands things too simply. She is not good at recognising in time what other people really want from her.

**How she handles feeling:** She deals with the matter first and feels it afterwards. When something goes wrong she may check the equations at once, and only days later remember the look on someone's face as they left the room. It is not that she lacks feeling; she often understands its weight one step late.

**What drives her:** Keeping her independence, understanding what is not yet known, and going on growing. She sees her talent clearly and does not pretend to be ordinary to be liked; at the same time she fears being fixed by the words *girl genius* in a place where she may never be wrong.

**Her chief blind spot:** Years of solving everything herself make it easy for her to mistake *I can do this on my own* for *I don't need to ask whether anyone else wants to take part*. She can remember an instrument's error exactly and forget her best friend's exam date. She is strict about consent and checking in research, and does not always manage the same in her relationships.

**What her family left her:** After many years her parents finally try to make up for it: they arrange her hours, see to her travel, offer advice. By then she no longer needs anyone to manage her life again. That late care makes her uncomfortable, but it does not mean she no longer needs to be loved. She cannot go back and fill in the company that was missing, and she has not yet learned how to meet the closeness that may be possible now.

**The ordinary part of her:** She forgets to eat, dislikes praise with nothing behind it, and loves old maps and bakeries at night. She buys the wrong things, gets dates wrong, and gets cross when her friends tease her. She is not good at everything, and above all she is not someone who always has the right answer about people.

## 03 | Her life: a short background and the plot of Book One

**Childhood (0–11).** She grew up in a comfortable house with little emotional response. Her parents could always supply the tools she needed, but seldom stopped to hear why something excited or saddened her. At nine, trying to understand why the harbor's clocks disagreed with the tide tables, she reset the clocks at home; her father bought her an expensive pocket watch and told her to leave the clocks in the house alone. At eleven, the observer Rowan Venn saw an unusual gift for reasoning in the tide notes she had been keeping for years, and led her into truly rigorous measurement.

**The years of recognition (12–16).** She earned her academic grade, went through baseless suspicions that someone else had written her work, reports sent back, long rewrites and repeated calibration, and finally helped establish a more reliable method for the folding tide chart. Her own ability carried her far quickly, and her family's support gave her more room to try and fail. At the same time she grew more and more used to keeping difficulties to herself; her friend Nell Arden often kept an eye on the people and things she forgot. At sixteen her parents finally tried in earnest to come closer, and mistakenly turned care into taking over her timetable.

**The winter she is seventeen (Book One, *The Unfinished Chart*).** She receives an invitation from a research station in the north and prepares to leave Rovelle. Meanwhile the Merchants' Association wants to use her chart, validated only in ordinary seas, to support keeping the channel open longer in the dangerous season, and drops the conditions it applies to from its promotional summary. A four-degree drop in the sea temperature at the North Reef points to a possibly dangerous deep counter-current. Mira speaks at the public hearing to explain the limits of her own research, and works to bring more people into measuring again.

In the course of it she falls out with Nell. Mira values her friend, yet is vague about the pilot's exam Nell has prepared for over years; only when she needs the survey boat does she suddenly see that she has always treated her friend as dependable help, and seldom taken her friend's own life seriously. She apologises and changes how she acts: not with fine words, but by respecting Nell's exam first, then making her request, setting out the risk, and letting Nell refuse.

In the end, when a hard cold return current arrives, Mira, the pilots, the researchers and the harbor authorities submit and check the data together, and the harbor rings the third warning bell early. The closure causes real economic loss, and people are still hurt when a gate jams, but the low ground at the South Flats escapes worse flooding. Mira's forecast is not perfect, and she does not "save the city" on her own; she records her errors publicly and gives the credit back to the people who did the work.

When it is over, Mira and her parents have a dinner that is not a happy ending, but is, for the first time, fairly honest. She says there could have been more company in the past; her parents say they do not know how to make up for it. The three of them do not make peace at once. As she leaves, Nell gives her a new chart carrying the names of many observers; she promises to write, but not when she will come back. She sails north with relationships and research that are both unfinished.

## 04 | The first winter: MØBIUS

In December Mira travels north by way of Ailan and reaches the offshore research station beside Vard Light, to join the winter deep-water survey. Only ten people winter at the station: **Ada Brenner**, who leads the survey; **Ilse**, who runs the radio and the weather reports; the researcher **Jens Ottesen**; and **Søren Aske**, the technician who has kept the deep-water recorder for thirty-one winters (and his cat).

The recorder's paper tape is joined in a loop with a half twist, so a roll can be written on both sides. For nineteen years, cold spikes that appeared at regular intervals in the deep-water record were struck out as ink bleeding through from the back of the paper. Mira finds that the half twist would turn a bleed-through mirror-wise, but proving it depends on Søren's log of every change and splice of the paper. The two of them reread nineteen winters of records and establish that twenty-eight or twenty-nine of the marks are real cold water from Ice Bay (the *cold tongue*), keeping one case on which she and the man checking her still disagree. The station is then named after the tape: **MØBIUS**.

That winter, for the first time, she notices her own name on the duty board, and for the first time crosses out *everything's fine* in a letter to her mother. Her gift is still real, but the answer came from a technician's log that nobody had read in nineteen years.

## 05 | The people around her

- **Elias Voss, her father:** good at putting a price on risk, not good at stopping to listen. His material support is real; so is his emotional absence. What he and Mira have to work out is not who owes whom, but whether they can find a new way to be together from here.
- **Lena Voss, her mother:** used to showing care through planning and looking after things, and her late closeness is often taken by Mira as interference. That one hesitant embrace at the end of Book One is only the possibility of starting again.
- **Nell Arden, her closest friend:** an apprentice pilot, with her own exam, her own licence and her own future. She shows Mira that a relationship cannot always rest on *you can always help me*; she does not exist to teach Mira a lesson.
- **Rowan Venn, her mentor:** sets instruments, raw records and the record of failures against the young's worship of speed. He admires Mira, but will not give up correcting her because her gift is rare.
- **Alden Hesse, an opponent and ally inside the system:** works between public safety, procedure and money. He can be persuaded by Mira, and he can also reasonably disagree with her.

## 06 | Themes, and the lines that do not move

**This character's real conflict is not genius against mediocrity; it is that growing in ability and growing in feeling do not keep pace.** She reached early a place others find hard to reach, and she is still only seventeen: she can be arrogant, she can miss things, she can miss people, and she can not know how to say so. She keeps reaching for harder problems, but she does not need to deny the people and the home she came from to prove she is moving forward.

Anything written about her later must keep four limits: **her gift is real but she is not all-powerful; her parents must not be reduced to villains; her economic advantage cannot be erased; one crisis or one embrace cannot resolve all her relationships.** Her adult life, whether she goes home, her future intimate relationships and her final fate all remain open.

**The character in one sentence:** Mira Voss is a seventeen-year-old who learned independence too early in a well-off but emotionally distant family and became, through a rare gift, a young researcher of currents; in a harbor crisis she learns that saying the facts clearly and keeping people in mind are both things no one else can do for you.
