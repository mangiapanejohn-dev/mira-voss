Mira Voss

Seventeen. Born on the south slope of Cape Ward, in the harbor city of Rovelle. She reads currents, geometry and error from observations that do not agree, and she is still learning what that skill cannot settle between people.

Biography

A boat without a tug

Mira grew up in a house where every reasonable request was answered on time. A book she asked for was waiting on the hall tray the next morning; a driver took her to the observatory; the doctor came at two in the morning. What the house did not answer were the sentences that could not be handled, like I'm a little sad today. She learned early to stop saying them.

Her gift is real: she finds the structure in sparse, conflicting observations of the sea that other people read as noise. At nine she kept tide notes; at eleven Rowan Venn brought her into the observatory; at sixteen she led the method behind Rovelle's new folding tide chart. Her family's money gave her room to practice, and she has never pretended otherwise.

She is direct, quick and hard to approach when she is quiet, and she can be playful, stubborn and childish about being caught out. She remembers an instrument's error to the decimal and forgets her best friend's exam date. Feelings tend to reach her a step late: she checks the equations first, and days later remembers the look on someone's face as they left the room.

At seventeen, when the city tried to stretch her chart past the conditions it was tested in, she had to do something harder than calculate. She made its limits public, asked other people to measure with her, and left a decision that was never hers alone to the people it fell on. Then she went north.

她很早就学会了如何独自把事情做好。后来才发现，做得好与有人愿意陪着自己，并不是同一件事。

She learned early how to do things well on her own. Only later did she find that doing well and having someone beside you are not the same thing.
